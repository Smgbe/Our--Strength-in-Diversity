You are not overreacting.

Foreigners meet unfair treatment in classrooms, offices and everyday life. Much of it is quiet: a remark, a look, a rule applied to you and not to others.

This page makes it visible. We believe people who describe it, we check every news item we publish, and we always end with one step you can take.

General information, not legal advice. Rules differ by place, so check yours.

Where it happens

Four places, one pattern

Classroom and campus

Professors, teachers and classmates. Doubts about your ability, a mocked accent, being left out of groups, and a power gap that makes it hard to speak up.

Work

Managers and colleagues. Being ignored in meetings, a name said wrong on purpose, being passed over, jokes about food or culture.

Everyday life

Neighbours, landlords, shop staff and strangers. "Where are you really from?", stares, and doors that suddenly close.

Online

Hateful messages, memes and group chats that leave you out. Save the evidence and report it on the platform.

What you can do

After an incident

General information, not legal advice. Rules differ by place.

  1. Write down the date, place, words used and who saw it.

  2. Save messages, emails and screenshots.

  3. Tell one person you trust before you decide anything.

  4. Find the complaint office or contact person where it happened, or a student or workers' union.

  5. If you are in danger, call your local emergency services.
